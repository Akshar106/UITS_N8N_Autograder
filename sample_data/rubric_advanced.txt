Assignment: Data Structures & Algorithms Midterm Review

Q1 (10 points): Explain what a binary search tree (BST) is, and state its average-case and worst-case time complexity for search. Full credit requires: (a) correct definition (left subtree values less than the node, right subtree values greater), (b) average-case O(log n), (c) worst-case O(n) with an explanation of when this happens (e.g. inserting already-sorted data producing a degenerate, linked-list-like tree).

Q2 (10 points): Compare recursion and iteration for computing the nth Fibonacci number. Full credit requires: (a) naive recursive time complexity of O(2^n) due to repeated subproblem recomputation, (b) recursive space complexity of O(n) from the call stack, (c) iterative time O(n) and space O(1), (d) mention of memoization/dynamic programming as an improvement to the recursive approach.

Q3 (10 points): What is Big-O notation and why is it used? Give one real example of an O(n log n) algorithm. Full credit requires: (a) Big-O describes the upper bound / worst-case growth rate of an algorithm as input size grows, (b) it lets you compare algorithms independent of hardware and constant factors, (c) a correct O(n log n) example such as merge sort or quicksort (average case).

Q4 (10 points): Explain the difference between an array and a linked list, including at least one advantage and one disadvantage of each. Full credit requires: array advantages (contiguous memory, O(1) random access, cache-friendly) and disadvantage (costly resize / fixed size); linked list advantages (O(1) insertion/deletion given a reference to the node) and disadvantage (O(n) access by index, extra pointer memory overhead).

Q5 (10 points): Describe how a hash table handles collisions. Compare separate chaining and open addressing. Full credit requires: (a) definition of a collision (two keys hashing to the same index), (b) separate chaining stores a list/bucket of entries per index, (c) open addressing probes for the next open slot (linear, quadratic, or double hashing), (d) at least one tradeoff between the two approaches (e.g. chaining handles high load factor more gracefully; open addressing has better cache locality but complicates deletion).
