# Grading Rubric — Module Two Assignment 2
## Electricity Consumption: Preprocessing and Regression

**Total: 20 points**

### How to use this rubric

This rubric is the grading authority. There is no answer key. Each criterion
says what the work must *demonstrate*, not what it must say, and several steps
have more than one correct implementation.

Four rules apply throughout:

1. **Any correct approach earns the marks.** The choice of scaler, encoding,
   correlation measure, train/test split, degree of polynomial and plotting
   library are all the student's to make. Do not deduct for choosing differently
   from another student or from your own preference.
2. **"Verify and handle" is satisfied by verifying and finding nothing.** A
   student who checks for duplicates or missing values, reports that the dataset
   has none, and moves on has done the step. Requiring them to invent a fix for
   a problem the data does not have would be wrong.
3. **A low accuracy score is not a wrong answer.** Weather variables explain only
   a small share of power consumption. Grade whether the model was built and
   evaluated correctly and whether the result was interpreted honestly — never
   the size of R².
4. **Code must run.** A cell that was never executed, or that ends in a
   traceback, has not produced the result it claims. Judge the code on its
   merits and say so in the comment, but do not credit an output that does not
   exist.

---

## Step 1. Data preprocessing — 5 points

The assignment states that neglecting preprocessing costs marks, so this is
scored across its five named requirements rather than as one judgment.

- **1 pt** — **Duplicated rows.** Checks for repeated rows (e.g. `duplicated()`)
  and either removes them or states that none were found.
- **1 pt** — **Missing values.** Checks for missing values (e.g. `isnull().sum()`)
  and either handles them by a stated method or states that none were found.
- **1 pt** — **Categorical to numerical.** Handles `Date Time`, which is the only
  non-numeric column. Any defensible treatment earns the mark: parsing it and
  extracting components (hour, month, day of week), converting to a numeric
  timestamp, or dropping it with a stated reason. Silently leaving a string
  column in the feature matrix does not.
- **1 pt** — **Feature scaling.** Applies a scaler to the numeric predictors, or
  argues that scaling is unnecessary for ordinary least squares. Both earn the
  mark; doing neither does not.
- **1 pt** — **Feature selection.** Selects features, or states on the evidence
  of the correlation analysis that all predictors are retained. The requirement
  says "if necessary", so a reasoned decision either way is full credit; silence
  is not.

*Award each of the five independently. A student who does four of the five
scores 4.*

---

## Step 2. Multicollinearity — 3 points

- **2 pts** — **Analysis.** Produces a correlation matrix over the predictors
  AND a visualization of it (a heatmap, or a pair plot / scatter matrix). Award
  1 if the matrix is computed but never visualized, or visualized but never
  computed.
- **1 pt** — **Interpretation.** Names which variables are collinear with which,
  from their own output, and says what that implies for regression (unstable or
  uninterpretable coefficients, redundant predictors). A plot with no reading of
  it earns 0 here.

*A student who additionally computes VIF has gone beyond the requirement. That
is welcome but earns no extra marks, and its absence costs none.*

---

## Step 3. Simple linear regression — 4 points

The assignment requires **two** separate single-feature models, then a
comparison.

- **1 pt** — **Feature choice.** Identifies two features as the "best" linear
  predictors AND ties the choice to the correlation analysis from Step 2. Any
  pair that follows from their own numbers is correct. A pair chosen with no
  stated reason earns 0.
- **2 pts** — **Two models built and evaluated.** Fits a separate simple linear
  regression on each of the two features and reports an accuracy score (R², or
  another metric the student names) for each. Award 1 if only one model was
  built, or if two were built but only one was scored.
- **1 pt** — **Visualization and comparison.** Plots the fit for the
  feature(s) — a scatter of the data with the regression line is the expected
  form — and states which of the two scored better.

---

## Step 4. Polynomial regression — 3 points

- **2 pts** — **Built and evaluated.** Fits a polynomial regression, states the
  degree used, and reports an accuracy score. Award 1 if the model is fitted but
  not scored, or scored but the degree is never stated.
- **1 pt** — **Visualization.** Plots the polynomial fit against the data.

*Any degree is acceptable. A student who tries several degrees and reports how
the score changed has done more than was asked, which is welcome but not
required.*

---

## Step 5. Multiple linear regression — 3 points

- **2 pts** — **Built and evaluated.** Fits a regression on multiple predictors
  and reports an accuracy score.
- **1 pt** — **Visualization.** Any defensible way of visualizing a
  multi-predictor fit earns the mark: predicted against actual, a residual plot,
  or a bar chart of coefficients. Do not require a form the assignment did not
  name.

---

## Step 5a. Comparison of the three models — 2 points

- **2 pts** — Compares simple, polynomial and multiple regression against each
  other using **their own reported scores**, and explains *why* the results came
  out in that order — for example that multiple regression uses more information
  than a single feature, or that a polynomial fits curvature a straight line
  cannot, or that a higher degree began to overfit.
- **1 pt** — Lists the three scores side by side with no explanation of the
  difference, or explains a difference that their own numbers do not show.
- **0** — No comparison, or a comparison of fewer than three models.

*The conclusion is not fixed. Whichever model came out ahead in the student's own
run is the right answer for them, provided the explanation matches their
numbers.*

---

### Cross-cutting deductions

These apply on top of the criteria above and are the only grounds for deducting
outside them:

- **Explanations.** The submission requirements ask for thorough explanations of
  graphs rather than one-liners. Where a criterion above awards a mark for
  interpretation, a single unexplained sentence does not earn it.
- **Code that does not run.** If a cell was never executed or ended in an error,
  the marks that depend on its output are not earned. Marks for code that is
  correct but unexecuted may still be awarded where the criterion is about
  method rather than result — say which applies in the comment.

---

### What the data does and does not fix
There is no answer key for this assignment and no single correct
implementation. Grade against the rubric criteria.

What is fixed by the data: the target variable is **Power consumption**;
**Date Time** is the only non-numeric column, so it is the one that must be
converted or dropped; the five remaining predictors are Temperature, Humidity,
Wind Speed, General diffuse flows and Diffuse flows.

What is **not** fixed, and must be judged from the student's own output rather
than assumed: whether this copy of the file contains duplicated rows or missing
values at all, and which features turn out to be the two "best" for simple
regression. A student who checks for duplicates and missing values, reports that
there are none, and moves on has completed that requirement — the requirement is
to verify and handle, and "none found" is a valid outcome of verifying. Likewise,
the two features chosen for simple regression need only follow from the
correlation analysis the student actually performed.

Accuracy on this dataset is expected to be poor for simple regression — weather
variables explain only a small share of power consumption, and a low R² that the
student reports honestly and explains is a correct result, not a failed one.
Do not reward a high score or penalise a low one; grade the method and the
interpretation.
